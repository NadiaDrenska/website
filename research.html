Research Interests

Exploring the intersection of PDEs, optimal control theory, and machine learning.

Research Interests

In my research, I use partial differential equations (PDEs), optimal control theory, and graph theory to analyze machine learning algorithms and investments, and give rigorous performance guarantees. I am a problem solver: I typically start with an application in mind, and learn and develop whatever methods I need in order to come up with a solution to the problem at hand. So far, I have bridged select ideas from viscosity solutions of PDEs, optimal control theory, and graph theory, to target problems in data science and investments. I have innovated and introduced continuous approaches to solve discrete problems from 'prediction with expert advice' and 'semi-supervised learning.' Moreover, my work proves that these approaches are asymptotically optimal.

A unifying theme for most of my research is adversarial two-player games, which arise in prediction problems as well as in p-Laplacian approaches to semi-supervised learning. My broader research goal is to use techniques from PDEs and ODEs to analyze and understand discrete problems from machine learning, graphs, and finance. Select applications are algorithm boosting, advertisement placing, stock investments, sports betting, self-driving car software, and data classification.

Another problem I have been working on is inferring body composition from 3D images. This project is joint with the Pennington Biomedical Research Center. You can find out more about it from the following article at SIAM Newsletter: Regression and Machine Learning Offer Insights About Body Composition Benchmarks

Another topic that I have worked on is machine learning algorithms for oyster seed counting. https://www.lsu.edu/science/news/science-next/2024/10/drenska.php

I also work on softball analytics. Here is more on this topic: https://www.lsu.edu/blog/2026/02/math-softball-research.php
